# Paper-01 文章介绍 & Cover Letter 草稿

---

## 一、文章介绍

### 这篇文章做了什么？

这是一篇**系统综述**，综合分析了过去20余年（2003–2026年）针对**健康老年人工作记忆训练迁移效果**的实证研究。

核心问题是：**老年人做工作记忆训练之后，认知能力能不能真正提升？在什么条件下更可能提升？**

最终纳入56篇研究，覆盖17个国家，结论是：训练本身有效，但迁移效果十分有限——63%的研究没有发现任何显著的组间迁移效应。

---

### 怎么做的？

按照PRISMA 2020标准流程：

1. **四库检索**（PubMed、PsycINFO、Web of Science、Scopus），共获得4,168条去重记录
2. **三轮筛选**：自动关键词过滤 → 题目摘要人工筛选 → 全文精读，最终纳入56篇
3. **数据提取**：57个字段，涵盖训练参数、样本特征、结局指标
4. **偏倚风险评估**：RCT用RoB 2.0（51篇），非随机研究用ROBINS-I（5篇）
5. **叙述性综合**（而非元分析）：因研究间异质性过高，无法合并效应量

---

### 为什么这么做？

**现有元分析的局限：**
Melby-Lervåg等人的元分析（2013、2016）已经表明训练迁移效果接近零，但这些分析主要针对混合年龄群体，且截止年份较早。**专门针对健康老年人、系统梳理调节因素**的综述至今缺失。

**本文的独特贡献：**
- 专注健康老年人（≥60岁），排除MCI/痴呆人群
- 系统考察五类潜在调节因素：训练剂量、是否有主动对照组、联合干预（如tDCS）、基线工作记忆水平、认知过程重叠度
- 时间跨度延伸至2026年，纳入最新研究
- 引入Barnett & Ceci（2002）的迁移距离分类框架分析近/远迁移差异

---

### 主要发现

| 维度 | 关键结论 |
|------|----------|
| 总体迁移 | 63% Null，21% Mixed，16% Positive |
| 有无主动对照 | 有主动对照组时Null率从25%升至74%，说明大量"阳性"结果是非特异性效应 |
| 联合干预（tDCS等） | 阳性率相对较高，但无法排除co-intervention本身的效果 |
| 训练剂量 | 无线性剂量-效应关系，>25次训练全部Null |
| 偏倚风险 | Low-RoB组阳性率50% vs SC组10%，提示方法学质量对结论影响显著 |

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled **"Working Memory Training in Healthy Older Adults: A Systematic Review of Transfer Effects, Training Parameters, and Potential Moderating Factors"** for consideration for publication in *[Journal Name]*.

**What this study addresses**

Working memory (WM) training has attracted considerable interest as a potential intervention to attenuate cognitive aging. However, despite a substantial empirical literature, the conditions under which WM training produces meaningful transfer to untrained cognitive domains remain poorly understood—particularly in healthy older adults. Prior meta-analyses have reported near-zero far-transfer effects when active control comparisons are applied, yet questions remain regarding which participant characteristics and training design features moderate these outcomes.

**What we did and found**

We conducted a systematic review following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, searching four databases (PubMed, PsycINFO, Web of Science, and Scopus) and including 56 randomized and quasi-randomized studies of WM training in healthy older adults (aged ≥60 years) published between 2003 and 2026. The review characterizes transfer outcomes across near- and far-transfer domains and synthesizes evidence on five potential moderating factors: training dose, active control group presence, combined interventions, baseline WM ability, and cognitive process overlap between training and transfer tasks.

Our central finding is that WM training reliably improves performance on trained criterion tasks but produces limited and inconsistent transfer to untrained cognitive domains: 63% of included studies found no significant between-group transfer effects, and positive outcomes were notably more common in studies with lower methodological rigor (50% positive among Low-RoB studies vs. 10% among studies with Some Concerns). These findings have direct implications for the design of future cognitive training interventions and for clinical expectations regarding the generalizability of WM training benefits in aging populations.

**Why this is appropriate for *[Journal Name]***

This manuscript falls squarely within the scope of *[Journal Name]* as a high-quality synthesis of empirical evidence on cognitive aging intervention. It addresses a clinically and theoretically important question for the aging field, provides a methodologically rigorous evidence base for both researchers and practitioners, and identifies specific gaps and priorities for future research. To our knowledge, this is the most comprehensive systematic review to date focusing specifically on transfer outcomes in healthy older adults, incorporating studies published through 2026.
