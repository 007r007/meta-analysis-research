## NOS Domain Scoring Rationale

### Selection (max 4)

Each study was scored on four sub-items (1 point each):
1. **Representativeness** of exposed cohort: community-based or large registry = 1; convenience/clinical = 0
2. **Selection** of non-exposed: drawn from same community = 1; other = 0
3. **Ascertainment** of exposure: structured interview or validated record = 1; self-report only = 0
4. **Demonstration** outcome of interest was not present at start of study: direct neural measurement = 1

*McKinnon (2023) scored 4: population-based birth cohort (TEBC), registry-linked exposure, structured assessment, neuroimaging at birth.*

### Comparability (max 2)

- 2 points: controls for at least two major confounders (e.g., child age/sex + household income or child cognitive ability)
- 1 point: controls for one major confounder only
- 0 points: no confounder control

*Wienke (2024) and Wijeakumar (2019) scored 1: each controlled age/sex but not a second key confounder (income or SES) independently.*

### Outcome (max 3)

1. **Assessment of outcome**: direct neuroimaging/EEG measure = 1; indirect proxy (IQ, behaviour) = 0
2. **Blinding**: assessors blind to exposure status = 1; not reported = 0
3. **Adequacy of follow-up** (longitudinal) OR **completeness of outcome data** (cross-sectional): attrition <20% or full neuroimaging dataset reported = 1

*McKinnon (2023) scored 3: direct MRI, assessor blinding documented (TEBC protocol), complete parcel-level dataset.*
*Ramphal (2020) scored 3: direct fMRI, blinded acquisition, longitudinal follow-up with <20% attrition.*
*Stiver (2015) scored 3: direct MRI, multi-timepoint, prospective cohort.*
*Ursache & Noble (2016) scored 3: direct DTI + executive function battery, controlled ascertainment.*
